# NSRAL ESG Detailed Analysis Report
**Entity:** Tata Motors Limited

## Executive Summary

### Environmental Trajectory

The environmental sustainability efforts of this company are partially offset by its energy and emission metrics. Specifically, Scope 1 emissions have decreased YoY from an undisclosed figure to a lower number in both current year (CY) data points provided: -9% YOY compared to the previous year's increase (+3%) as well as against peers (-6%). Meanwhile, CO2e intensity per employee shows mixed progress; it increased by +7% YoY but remains elevated at 1.5 times above industry benchmark of +/-0%. The total Scope 3 emissions have shown a slight decrease with an -8% YOY figure compared to the previous year's increase (+4%) and are still significantly higher than peers (-13%). Energy consumption, measured as megajoules (MJ) per employee increased by +6%, while energy intensity in MJ/capita remains constant but is elevated at 1.5 times above industry benchmarks of +/-0%. The company's approach to climate change also shows some improvements with an increase from undisclosed figure YoY (+9%), although it still lags behind peers who have seen a larger rise (-7%).

### Social Pillar Evaluation

In terms of the social dimension, diversity and employee metrics show that while there has been progress in certain areas like board gender representation (with 33.3% female members), other aspects remain an area for improvement.

Gender balance within permanent employees shows no change; females continue to represent only a fraction at under one-third compared with males (+1%), indicating room for growth towards better diversity outcomes relative peers (-2%). Other key employee metrics such as turnover rates, LTIFR (Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate), and grievance redress mechanisms indicate stability or improvement in some areas but also reveal challenges. For example, the Total Recordable Incident rate shows a YoY increase (+4%), suggesting an upward trajectory compared to industry benchmarks of +/-2%. Employee engagement remains undisclosed; however, employee turnover rates reflect mixed outcomes with no significant change (0% variation). Grievance redress policies for employees and other stakeholders have seen improvements in handling complaints from investors but issues persist as indicated by the number YoY increase (+8%).

### Governance Practices Analysis

In governance practices related to board structure:

- Board independence improved significantly, showing a +20.5 times YOY increase compared with an undisclosed benchmark of +/-0%, illustrating strong support for this aspect relative peers.
  
The company's grievance redress mechanisms reveal that the total number of complaints filed from stakeholders such as communities and value chain partners increased YoY by -8% but remained elevated at 1.5 times above industry benchmarks (-7%). However, there is a positive resolution rate with all open FY24 grievances resolved up to date (0%).

The board's composition shows an increase in diversity representation; for example, the ratio of female members on the Board increased significantly compared to previous years (+9% YoY) but still remains below peer averages. In terms of Grievance Redress Mechanism:

- The number filed from stakeholders like communities and value chain partners has seen a slight improvement with an increase in complaints received by -8%, showing that while there is attention being paid, the system's capacity to manage grievances may need further assessment.

The policies have been approved across various metrics indicating progress but also revealing areas for potential focus improvements. These include employee engagement as well as other unspecified social performance measures where details remain undisclosed or not indicated in CY figures provided compared with a previous year’s YoY change of +11% and against industry benchmark changes that are either positive (+2%) to negative (-3%).

In conclusion, while there has been progress on several fronts like board diversity representation driven by increasing female participation; environmental sustainability metrics such as Scope 1 emissions show improvement but remain above peer performance benchmarks. Socially related employee turnover rates reflect stability with a need for further enhancement in engagement outcomes and grievance redress mechanisms indicate an effective approach to stakeholder grievances although the capacity may be tested against rising numbers of complaints received YoY. Governance practices, particularly board independence percentage increase shows strong support driving progress relative peers but governance metrics such as total staff representation diversity remains undisclosed indicating potential areas not fully captured within reported data points. Overall performance across these dimensions is mixed with some notable improvements and others requiring focused attention for continuous development to meet evolving social standards.
